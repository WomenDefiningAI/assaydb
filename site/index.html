Correctness method and the 37-hour clock

Pilot and full test use different collections. Axes zoom to 31–37h and 90–100%; no earlier result is inferred. The eligible count excludes only control records with no SQL to run, records after a file’s halt instruction, and records marked only for another engine. These exclusions were fixed before execution. Unsupported SQL and unexpected execution or resource errors still count as failures; the full collection retains all records.

Full-run completion times are derived from manifest start time plus recorded elapsed seconds. The clock begins at the first recorded commit, which already contained early work. This is elapsed time, not active coding time or the full project duration. Passing chosen tests is not a proof of all possible inputs.

How we tested

The dates show when we documented each result, not its exact completion time. Points are equally spaced in recorded order. All seven use the same 0–4.5× total-time scale, but different computers and methods mean they do not show a controlled improvement. We do not connect them with a line.

The Alpha 3 target is 0.95× Polars for both total time and geometric mean. Total compares the time for the whole workload. Geometric mean gives each query equal weight when comparing time ratios. Neither measure alone establishes that we met the goal.

The Development Mini results are reported single-round diagnostics against historical Polars references. Their sampling-compatibility audit was outstanding. Corrected collector code does not establish that these older runs excluded warmup or retained five complete timed samples. We make no corrected-protocol compatibility claim. Noise-triggered retries were allowed; these summaries do not establish an unbiased sampling history.

The M2 Pro starting point uses a different three-round native-CLI protocol. It had background activity. It is distinct from the historical M4 result of 4.1364×. The chart notes below retain the exact measurement scopes and limits.

A separate provisional test on October 15

One M4 development-host attempt reported 0.970× Polars total time and 1.046× geometric mean. It is not accepted performance evidence and does not meet the 0.95× goal. It is separate from the chart’s Development Mini diagnostics and the Test Mini evidence.

The run used all 22 SF10 queries, 10 requested workers, three rotated rounds, one warmup and five timed samples per engine/query/round. Marginal 95% intervals were 0.967–0.973× total and 1.041–1.048× geometric mean, bootstrapped over three round clusters. Fifteen samples are not fifteen independent host trials.

The host was an Apple M4 with 16 GiB RAM, macOS 26.6.2 and Rust 1.90.0, with no custom RUSTFLAGS. Assay used a 4 GiB accounted budget and Polars used streaming. Resource settings are not equivalent whole-process caps. Quiet-host admission sampled six five-second intervals; the harness did not continuously record background activity.

Performance methods, quantitative goals and earlier results

This historical chart measures the alpha2 source-matched native CLI at revision fb72cc0 on Apple M2 Pro, against DuckDB 1.5.6 and Polars 2.0.0. It is not installed Python-wheel or Python-API timing.

Timing runs from SQL to a fully materialized Arrow result. Each value sums the per-query mean of three round medians; each median uses five hot samples after a warmup. This was a best-effort run with background activity, not a quiet-host baseline. These are research measurements, not audited TPC-H results.

The next goal: 0.95× or less against Polars for both total query time and geometric mean on the same roughly 10 GB, 22-query workload, with correctness checks retained and full settings and results recorded. That is a goal, not an achieved result or a promise about every query.

Earlier small-data results: an earlier engine on Apple M4 used 6.2% less total query time than Polars and 27.1% less than DuckDB at roughly 1 GB with one requested worker. That run is separate from the 37-hour correctness checkpoints and the historical alpha2/M2 Pro chart.

Historical timing summary (JSON)

Now, make 10 GB practical.

Early 1 GB performance showed promise. The larger workload exposes a gap, especially with more workers. We are optimizing toward Polars and DuckDB parity so the engine can be useful for local analysis.

Here, 1 GB and 10 GB mean approximate uncompressed dataset size, not memory use. Performance and production readiness are still work in progress.

Then, verify the riskiest parts of the engine.

Running optimization and proof work together has been inefficient for this project. We are focusing on performance first, then extending targeted verification of actual engine code, building on the arithmetic checks already done.
