Totals compare sums of query-round medians. Geometric means summarize paired per-query ratios and answer a different question. Each reviewed run uses three rounds, one warmup and five timed samples per query and round, with 1 or 10 requested workers. PR5 rotates engine order; ordinary release builds use debug=0, strip=none, without PGO. Sampled quiet-host checks cannot establish zero timing interference. Historical M2 Pro results use their own method and configuration.

Lessons from the speed log

Development milestones, separate from the measured bars above. These sanitized notes describe what changed; intermediate ratios are withheld until their host, worker count and protocol are confirmed. They do not establish Alpha 3.

  1. Use compact group identities to avoid repeated grouping work.

  2. A more suitable distinct-counting strategy reduced repeated work in a slow query.

  3. Avoid scanning and filtering the same input repeatedly.

  4. Optimization admission must reflect actual cardinality. Keep corrected measurements instead of choosing the first, more favorable timing.

  5. Improve how workers claim scan work; worker count alone does not guarantee useful parallelism.

  6. Scheduling changes need resource ownership and cancellation checks as well as timing. A matching committed tree does not itself prove a binary rebuild comparison.

  7. Total time and geometric mean can disagree. A provisional near-parity total from one attempt is not an accepted release result.

The next goal: 0.95× or less against Polars for both total query time and geometric mean on the same roughly 10 GB, 22-query workload, with correctness checks retained and full settings and results recorded. That is a goal, not an achieved result or a promise about every query.

Earlier small-data results: an earlier engine on Apple M4 used 6.2% less total query time than Polars and 27.1% less than DuckDB at roughly 1 GB with one requested worker. That run is separate from the 37-hour correctness checkpoints and the historical alpha2/M2 Pro chart.

Now, make 10 GB practical.

Early 1 GB performance showed promise. The larger workload exposes a gap, especially with more workers. We are optimizing toward Polars and DuckDB parity so the engine can be useful for local analysis.

Here, 1 GB and 10 GB mean approximate uncompressed dataset size, not memory use. Performance and production readiness are still work in progress.

Then, verify the riskiest parts of the engine.

Running optimization and proof work together has been inefficient for this project. We are focusing on performance first, then extending targeted verification of actual engine code, building on the arithmetic checks already done.
